FloodCrew: Project Proposal

A smart helper that tells a council where to send its crews and gritters, and warns the public, during floods, snow and black ice.

John Ajiboye · MSc AI with Data Analytics, Teesside University · Intelligent Decision Support Systems · November 2026

1. The problem

When a river floods or roads freeze, a council has only a few crews and gritters. Staff must decide quickly where to send them, often using guesswork or waiting for an official warning, which can be too late. A care home, school or hospital road can be left unprotected. Drivers and people walking also often find a flooded or icy road only when they reach it.

2. Aim

To build a smart system that helps a council protect the most vulnerable places before bad weather hits, send its crews and gritters to the right places, and keep the public informed.

3. Objectives

  1. Predict river levels 6 hours ahead and which roads will freeze overnight.
  2. Give every place and road a risk score (how likely × how bad).
  3. Suggest the best job for each crew and gritter automatically.
  4. Let the officer approve the big decisions and try "what if" scenarios.
  5. Send the public alerts for their street and a safer route.
  6. Test the system fairly against what councils do today.

4. The proposed solution

How FloodCrew works:

It follows Simon's decision model (intelligence, design, choice, review) and has three parts:

Officer dashboardMap, ready-made plan, approvals and what-if
Crew phone pageEach job and the reason for it, plus status updates
Public alerts pageAlerts by postcode, flooded and icy roads on a map, safer route

5. Who will use it

UserWhat they get

7. Tools and data

  • Data: Environment Agency open data (free) and Open-Meteo weather forecasts. Simulated River Tees data is used first while real data is collected.
  • Build: Python (scikit-learn, SciPy, FastAPI) and a web app.

8. How it will be tested

The models are trained on older data and tested on newer data they have never seen. FloodCrew is compared with today's practice: sending crews when the official warning comes, and gritting at fixed times. Early results on simulated data:

9. Expected benefits

  • Vulnerable places protected earlier.
  • Better use of a small number of crews and gritters.
  • Fewer people driving or walking into danger.
  • Clear, explainable decisions with a full record.

10. Risks and limits

  • Results so far use simulated data; real data is the next step.
  • Flood levels and gritting routes are estimates until the council's real ones are used.
  • Public alerts need officer approval to avoid false alarms. The system supports official warnings and does not replace them.
